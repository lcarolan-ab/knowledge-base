# Cash flow projection 2026–2035 — Kessler family trust, May 2026 revision

> **Synthetic demo data.** Invented family and figures.

**Supersedes** the November 2025 edition. That edition assumed no major purchases; in
March 2026 the trustees agreed to buy a lake house for **$1,900,000**, closing in
July 2026. This revision reflects the purchase and its carrying costs.

## What changed

| item | November 2025 | May 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Major purchases | none | lake house, $1,900,000, July 2026 |
| Annual spending | $260,000 | **$298,000** (adds $38,000 of carrying costs: tax, insurance, upkeep) |
| Taxable portfolio after funding | $8,400,000 | **$7,400,000** after a $1,000,000 liquidation |
| Net portfolio draw | $202,000 (2.1%) | **$295,000 (3.5%)** including $55,000 of interest |
| Verdict | sustainable indefinitely | **at the ceiling; sustainable only with the funding structure below** |

## Recommended funding structure

Paying the full $1,900,000 from the taxable portfolio would realise an estimated
**$210,000 of capital gains** (about $50,000 of tax) and push the draw above 4%.
Instead:

1. **Liquidate $1,000,000** from the taxable portfolio, choosing the highest-basis
   lots to keep realised gains near the $210,000 estimate or below it.
2. **Borrow $900,000 on a pledged-asset line** secured by the trust portfolio, at about
   6.1%, costing roughly $55,000 a year in interest. Repay from RMDs and portfolio
   income over five to seven years rather than selling further appreciated positions.
3. Keep the **$1,000,000 in cash from June** so the closing does not depend on market
   conditions in July.

## Projection under the recommended structure

- Net draw of **3.5%** in 2026 and 2027, falling to about **3.0%** by 2029 as the line
  is repaid and RMDs (from 2027, about $42,000 in the first year) cover part of spend.
- The portfolio's real value is roughly flat through 2030, then rises. This is a
  materially weaker position than the November edition showed, and the
  "sustainable indefinitely" conclusion of that edition **no longer applies**.
- **Roth conversions** of about $150,000 a year in 2026 and 2027 remain attractive:
  the trustees are in a low bracket before RMDs begin, and conversions reduce the RMDs
  that would otherwise be forced later.

## Recommendations

1. Adopt the liquidate-plus-borrow structure above.
2. Hold spending other than the house at the 2025 level; the $70,000 of headroom the
   November edition identified is gone.
3. Proceed with Roth conversions in 2026 and 2027.
4. Review annually; a second property or a spending increase would breach the ceiling.
