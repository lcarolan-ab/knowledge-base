# Cash flow projection

A multi-year projection of income, tax, spending and savings capacity, built from tax
returns, compensation statements, the household's card analysis and fixed-cost
schedules, with named scenarios and a recommendation list
[^abernathy-ruiz-cash-flow-2026]. Three are in the library: one for the Abernathy-Ruiz
household and two editions for the Kessler family trust [^synthesis].

## Who has one

| client | horizon | headline |
|---|---|---|
| [[clients/abernathy-ruiz]] | 2026–2030 | savings capacity $107,600, turning negative from 2029 in the school-plus-RSU-cliff scenario [^abernathy-ruiz-cash-flow-2026] |
| [[clients/kessler]] | 2026–2035, November 2025 edition | net draw 2.1%, sustainable indefinitely; **superseded** [^kessler-cash-flow-2025-11] |
| [[clients/kessler]] | 2026–2035, May 2026 revision | net draw 3.5% after the lake house, at the ceiling [^kessler-cash-flow-2026-05] |

## Conventions the projections share

- **A sustainability ceiling of 3.5%** net portfolio draw for retired households
  [^kessler-cash-flow-2025-11] [^kessler-cash-flow-2026-05].
- **Card spend is carried in from the card analysis** and, in the working-household
  case, held flat, which the projection itself names as its most fragile assumption
  [^abernathy-ruiz-cash-flow-2026].
- **Base returns of 5% nominal and 2.5% inflation** for the retired case
  [^kessler-cash-flow-2025-11].
- **Revisions supersede rather than amend.** The May 2026 Kessler revision states
  that the November edition's conclusion no longer applies and keeps a
  side-by-side table of what changed [^kessler-cash-flow-2026-05].

## How projections have held up

The one projection with a follow-up so far is the Kessler funding plan: the estimated
$210,000 of realised gains from a $1,000,000 liquidation came in at $118,000 because
the highest-basis lots were sold first, so the plan's tax cost was lower than projected
without any change to the plan [^kessler-cash-flow-2026-05]
[^kessler-performance-2026q2]. See [[topics/funding-a-large-purchase]] and
[[services/investment-performance-summary]].

No working-household projection has yet been checked against a later card analysis,
so whether the flat-spend assumption holds is an open question [^synthesis]. See
[[questions/open-questions]].
