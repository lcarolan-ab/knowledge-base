# Funding a large purchase

The library has one worked case, followed from projection to execution: the Kessler
family's $1,900,000 lake house, closing July 2026 [^kessler-cash-flow-2026-05].

## The choice

Paying the whole price from the taxable portfolio would have realised an estimated
$210,000 of gains and pushed the net draw above 4%, past the firm's 3.5% ceiling
[^kessler-cash-flow-2026-05]. The recommended structure was to liquidate $1,000,000,
selling the highest-basis lots, and borrow $900,000 on a pledged-asset line at about
6.1%, repaid from RMDs and portfolio income over five to seven years
[^kessler-cash-flow-2026-05]. Cash for the closing was to be raised in June so the
purchase did not depend on July markets [^kessler-cash-flow-2026-05].

## What happened

The trust sold $1,000,000 in June using specific-lot identification and realised
$118,000 of gains, well under the estimate, cutting the expected tax to about $28,000;
the line was established in June for drawing at closing [^kessler-performance-2026q2].
The cost was about 0.5% of year-to-date return in cash drag
[^kessler-performance-2026q2].

## What it cost the plan

The purchase moved the household from a 2.1% net draw with $70,000 of spending headroom
to a 3.5% draw with none, and the earlier "sustainable indefinitely" verdict was
withdrawn [^kessler-cash-flow-2025-11] [^kessler-cash-flow-2026-05]. See
[[clients/kessler]], [[services/cash-flow-projection]] and
[[services/investment-performance-summary]].
