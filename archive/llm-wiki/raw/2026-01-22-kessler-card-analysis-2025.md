# Credit card spend analysis — Kessler family, CY2025

> **Synthetic demo data.** Invented family and figures.

**Prepared for:** Ilse and Tomas Kessler
**Scope:** two personal credit cards, 1 Jan – 31 Dec 2025
**Method:** statement download, categorisation under standard v2, manual review of
charges over $1,000.

## Headline

Total card spend was **$96,300**. Travel dominates because of a six-week trip to
Europe in May and June.

| category | 2025 spend | share |
|---|---|---|
| Travel | $42,400 | 44% |
| Home & garden | $17,300 | 18% |
| Dining | $11,600 | 12% |
| Medical & pharmacy | $10,600 | 11% |
| Grandchildren (gifts, activities) | $5,800 | 6% |
| Subscriptions | $3,100 | 3% |
| Other | $5,500 | 6% |
| **Total** | **$96,300** | 100% |

## Findings

1. The Europe trip accounts for **$28,100** of travel. It was paid on a card with a
   3% foreign transaction fee and no primary travel insurance, costing about $840 in
   fees and leaving a cancelled rail segment uncovered.
2. **Subscriptions.** 14 recurring charges, of which four are unused (a second
   streaming service, a roadside-assistance plan duplicated by the auto insurer, and
   two app subscriptions). Cancelling them saves **$860 a year**.
3. **Disputed charges.** Two fraudulent charges totalling **$1,140** were identified
   in August, disputed, and recovered in full.
4. **Rewards.** 180,000 points are unredeemed, worth about **$2,700** at 1.5 cents
   per point.
5. Medical spend is stable and largely reimbursable; $2,200 of eligible receipts had
   not been submitted to the supplemental plan.

## Recommendations

- Move travel spend to a card with no foreign transaction fee and primary travel
  insurance before the 2026 trips.
- Cancel the four unused subscriptions ($860 a year).
- Submit the $2,200 of outstanding medical receipts.
- Redeem the 180,000 points against 2026 travel.
