# Cash flow projection 2026–2030 — Abernathy-Ruiz household

> **Synthetic demo data.** Invented household and figures, for demonstration only.

**Prepared for:** Dana Abernathy and Marco Ruiz
**Horizon:** five years, 2026–2030, annual resolution
**Inputs:** 2025 tax return, compensation statements, the 2025 credit card spend
analysis, mortgage and insurance schedules.

## Base case

| line | 2026 |
|---|---|
| Salaries (Dana, Marco) | $480,000 |
| RSU vesting (Dana; grant fully vests March 2028) | $132,000 |
| **Gross income** | **$612,000** |
| Income and payroll taxes | ($186,000) |
| Card spend (carried from the 2025 card analysis) | ($187,400) |
| Non-card spend: mortgage $62,000, insurance $14,000, childcare and tuition $31,000, utilities and other $24,000 | ($131,000) |
| **Total spend** | **($318,400)** |
| **Savings capacity** | **$107,600** |

Assumptions: discretionary card spend held **flat at the 2025 level** of $187,400;
non-card spend inflates at 3% a year; salaries grow 3%; RSU vesting continues at
$132,000 a year until the March 2028 cliff and then stops.

## Scenarios

- **Independent school for both children from 2027** adds $64,000 a year. Savings
  capacity falls to about **$43,600**.
- **RSU cliff (2028).** After March 2028 the $132,000 of annual vesting ends. With
  the school scenario in place, savings capacity turns **negative (about −$88,000)
  from 2029** unless spend adjusts or a new equity grant is made.
- **Base case only.** Without the school change, capacity stays positive through
  2030 even after the RSU cliff, at about $20,000 a year from 2029.

## Recommendations

1. Maximise both 401(k) plans ($47,000 combined in 2026).
2. Fund the two 529 plans at $30,000 a year in total while RSU income lasts.
3. Direct a further $30,000 a year to the taxable account.
4. Hold six months of total spend (about $160,000) in cash.
5. Revisit the plan at the RSU cliff, and earlier if the school decision is made.
6. The flat-spend assumption is the most fragile input. Card spend grew 9% in 2025;
   if that continues, capacity falls by roughly $17,000 a year, compounding.
