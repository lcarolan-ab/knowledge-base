**Prepared for:** Ilse (68) and Tomas (72) Kessler, trustees
**Horizon:** ten years, 2026–2035

## Position

| item | value |
|---|---|
| Kessler Family Trust, taxable portfolio | $8,400,000 |
| Tomas's IRA | $1,100,000 |
| **Total investable** | **$9,500,000** |
| Annual spending | $260,000 |
| Social Security (both) | $58,000 |
| **Net portfolio draw** | **$202,000 (2.1% of investable)** |

## Projection

- The net draw of 2.1% is well under the firm's 3.5% sustainability ceiling.
- Required minimum distributions from Tomas's IRA begin in 2027, when he turns 73,
  and are projected at about $42,000 in the first year. They exceed the IRA's share of
  spending, so part of each RMD will be reinvested in the taxable account.
- Under the base return assumption (5% nominal, 2.5% inflation) the portfolio's real
  value **rises in every scenario tested**, including a 20% equity drawdown in 2027.
- **No major purchases are assumed.** The trustees indicated none were planned.

## Conclusion

The plan is sustainable indefinitely at current spending. There is room for either
increased spending of up to $70,000 a year or accelerated gifting to the grandchildren
without breaching the ceiling.

## Recommendations

1. Keep spending at or below $330,000 a year (the level at which the draw reaches
   3.5%).
2. Begin Roth conversions from Tomas's IRA in 2026 to reduce future RMDs.
3. Revisit if spending, health, or a large purchase changes the picture.
